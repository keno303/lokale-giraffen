Field Guide • Giraffe Locomotion & Anatomy

The Pacing Gait (Lateral Locomotion): Giraffes are true pacers. Unlike horses or dogs which walk with diagonal foot pairs, giraffes move both legs on the same side forward almost synchronously. This prevents their exceptionally long hind legs (up to 1.9m) from colliding with their front legs during each stride.
Pendulum Neck Counterbalance: The iconic 2-meter long neck acts as a dynamic physical pendulum. During each forward stride, the neck and heavy head (up to 45 kg) dip forward and down to pull the center of gravity over the stance legs, bobbing twice per complete stride cycle and leaning laterally to balance the ipsilateral shift.
7 Cervical Vertebrae: Like all mammals (including humans), giraffes have exactly 7 neck vertebrae. However, each giraffe vertebra can exceed 28 cm (11 inches) in length, articulated with deep ball-and-socket joints for extraordinary rotational flexibility.
Reticulated Coat Pattern: The polygonal spots separated by creamy white channels are not only camouflage among acacia shadows; each patch contains an underlying complex network of thermal blood vessels that act as biological radiators to dissipate African heat.
Cloven Hooves & High Hock: Giraffe hooves are cloven (split into two weight-bearing digits) measuring up to 30 cm across. The prominent "backward knee" on the hind leg is actually the anatomical hock (ankle/calcaneus), elevated high above the ground on a massive cannon bone.
